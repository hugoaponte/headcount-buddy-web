# Courts and Scores at a Scrimmage

Headcount Buddy can track what happened on each court at a scrimmage — who played whom, how it went, and what the score was. Planning courts ahead of time is completely optional. Many groups just show up, play, and text in the scores afterwards. Both ways work.

---

## Planning courts before play (optional)

If you want to write down the matchups ahead of time, text them in naturally:

> **Hugo:** "Sunday: court 1 Hugo/Amol vs Neil/Steve, court 2 Ana/Ben vs Cara/Dan"
>
> **Assistant:** "Set for Sunday 9am — Court 1: Hugo/Amol vs Neil/Steve · Court 2: Ana/Ben vs Cara/Dan. 8 games, ties ok. Want me to let everyone know?"

That's all it takes. Players can then ask which court they're on:

> **Ana:** "Who am I playing with Sunday?"
>
> **Assistant:** "Court 2: you and Ben vs Cara and Dan, 8 games."

If someone you name isn't on the roster, they'll be kept as a guest:

> **Hugo:** "Court 1 is Hugo/Amol vs Jen/Steve"
>
> **Assistant:** "Got it — Court 1: Hugo/Amol vs Jen/Steve (Jen isn't on the roster — kept her as a guest)."

If you need to redo the courts entirely, say so:

> **Hugo:** "Scrap that, redo the courts: court 1 Hugo/Neil vs Amol/Steve, court 2 same"

The old plan is replaced. Any courts that already have scores reported are left untouched.

## Reporting scores after play

This is the part that matters most. Anyone who played — or the organizer — can text in the scores in plain language, all at once or one court at a time.

### A single court

> **Amol:** "We won 5-3"
>
> **Assistant:** "Got it — Court 1: Hugo/Amol d. Neil/Steve 5-3. Anything to correct?"

The assistant figures out which court Amol was on and which side he was reporting for. The reply always reads winner first.

### Multiple courts in one text

> **Hugo:** "Court 1 5-3 us, court 2 they took it 5-4"
>
> **Assistant:** "Court 1: Hugo/Amol d. Neil/Steve 5-3 · Court 2: Cara/Dan d. Ana/Ben 5-4. Anything to correct?"

One text, both courts recorded.

### A combination nobody planned

No pre-set courts? No problem. If the people who played weren't in the plan — or there was no plan at all — just name who was on each side:

> **Ana:** "Cara and I played Ben and Dan instead, we won 6-2"
>
> **Assistant:** "Got it — Ana/Cara d. Ben/Dan 6-2 (that one wasn't on the plan, added it). Anything to correct?"

### A court you didn't get to

> **Hugo:** "We never got to court 2"
>
> **Assistant:** "Noted — court 2 wasn't played."

### A score with no plan at all

If you ran a pickup scrimmage with nothing planned and want to text in several results at once, just describe what happened:

> **Hugo:** "Hugo/Amol beat Neil/Steve 5-3, then Ana/Ben beat Cara/Dan 5-3"
>
> **Assistant:** "Court 1: Hugo/Amol d. Neil/Steve 5-3 · Court 2: Ana/Ben d. Cara/Dan 5-3. Anything to correct?"

---

## How the assistant works out the details

**Which event?** The assistant figures it out from context — you don't need to name the event. If two scrimmages could fit, it will ask once to confirm which one you mean.

**Who won?** When you name both sides, whoever you name first (or whoever you say won) is the winner. When you just say "we won," it uses your own court as the reference. If you're reporting a court you weren't on and don't name who won, it will ask you once.

**The format?** The assistant reads the format from the numbers — one set, best of three, a 10-point tiebreak, an 8-game set. If a court was planned with a specific format, that's used as the starting point.

**Scoring a second match on the same court?** If the same court is reported again with different players or another round, the assistant keeps it as the next round on that court rather than overwriting the first result.

## Making corrections

The read-back after every report is what lets you catch a mistake fast. If something's off, just describe what needs changing — no need to clear anything and start over:

> **Hugo:** "Court 1 was 6-3 not 6-2"
>
> **Assistant:** "Updated — Court 1: Hugo/Amol d. Neil/Steve 6-3. Anything else?"

> **Hugo:** "Replace Amol with this number: [phone]"
>
> **Assistant:** "Done — swapped Amol for [name] on all of tonight's courts, scores untouched."

> **Hugo:** "That was full sets, not 8 games"
>
> **Assistant:** "Got it — updated the format on court 1. Anything else?"

You can correct any part of a line: the score, the players, or the format. Name the court or the line you're fixing, and the rest stays as it was.

---

## If someone else already reported a court

If a teammate already filed a result and you send something different, the assistant will show you both and ask before changing anything:

> **Steve:** "Court 1 was 5-3 us"
>
> **Assistant:** "Amol already reported court 1 as Hugo/Amol d. Neil/Steve 5-3 — want me to replace that with yours?"

It won't overwrite another person's report without a word unless you're the organizer.

---

## Clearing the night's scores

If you need to wipe the scores and start fresh, the organizer can say so:

> **Hugo:** "Clear all the scores from tonight"
>
> **Assistant:** "Done — all scores cleared. Courts are back to planned, ready for new reports."

This resets just the scores. The planned matchups stay in place.

## The event page

The link in your confirmation and headcount texts goes to an event page where you can also see and manage courts. It shows:

- **Before play:** the matchups planned, if any were set
- **After play:** the results, winner first, with the format

If you're a confirmed player (from a few hours before play) or the organizer, you can add or correct a court directly on the page. Pick the two sides from the people who are in — guests show up under whoever brought them — type the score with side A first, and the format is read from the numbers unless you choose one. The organizer can also remove a court. Everything you enter there writes the same record as a text.

---

## A few things worth knowing

- **Planning is optional.** A pickup scrimmage with nothing planned works exactly the same — scores come in, courts are created, the read-back shows what happened.
- **Short sessions are fine.** If you ran out of court time and only got to 4-3, that's a complete result — whoever was ahead won it. No warning, no problem.
- **Scores feed reports.** If your team has post-scrimmage reports turned on, the scores recorded here flow into them automatically. See the reports page for details.
- **Names the assistant doesn't recognize** are kept exactly as written. A friend who filled in is "Hugo's guest" if Hugo brought them.
