# Headcount Buddy help

The front door to the guides — a short, friendly orientation to what the assistant does, then a clean numbered list linking every topic page.

## 1. [START, STOP, and INFO](basic-flows.md)

The canonical text keywords every teammate can use — START to opt in, STOP to opt out, INFO/HELP for a quick reminder of who we are. Plain, factual, and exact about what each one does.

## 2. [Getting help](help.md)

How to get unstuck — ask the assistant a question in plain text, share feedback or report a problem, and reach a human when you need one.

## 3. [What we support: definitions and scope](scope.md)

A plain-English glossary of the building blocks — the sports we support (tennis today), the kinds of groups (formal teams and informal pickup), the event types, the gender formats for play, the RSVP states, and the organizer roles. Ground every list in the code so it can't drift.

## 4. [About: cost, access, and who builds Headcount Buddy](about.md)

The straight answers to the basics people ask. Order the page this way: LEAD with who builds Headcount Buddy, then how to get access, and END with what it costs. Plain and confident; no hedging, no "I'm not sure." These are facts, so state them directly.

## 5. [Getting your team or group on board](teams.md)

A captain or organizer gets their team or group on board by reaching out to us — we're in a limited alpha today, so onboarding is hands-on and we set each team up together. Covers the sports and group types we support: tennis today, with both formal teams and informal pickup groups welcome.

## 6. [Your first text from the assistant](start.md)

What a player should expect the first time the assistant texts them — what it is, what it can help with, and how it catches them up on anything they missed before they joined. You never install anything.

## 7. [Building your roster](roster.md)

How a captain or organizer adds teammates so the assistant can reach them. The assistant can ask players directly for the details it needs — like a name, or gender for mixed-format play — taking that task off the captain's shoulders instead of leaving them to chase it down.

## 8. [Asking your team to RSVP](get-rsvps.md)

How a captain or organizer asks the whole team to RSVP for a scrimmage or practice — one text, no @-everyone group-chat noise. The assistant collects replies, automatically chases holdouts as the event nears, and lets the organizer send an on-demand nudge ("remind the maybes", "nudge Victor") anytime. Also cover that, while an event is still days out, the assistant sends the organizer a light batched progress update as replies come in — and that this update shows BOTH the headcount so far AND how many of the roster the assistant has already reached out to, e.g. "you're at 5 of 8, and I've messaged 6 of your 8 players" — never a per-reply ping. Lead with how little the organizer has to do. (The automatic reminder schedule itself is covered on the reminders page.)

## 9. [Replying to RSVPs](rsvp.md)

How a player RSVPs by text — yes/no/maybe plus freeform constraints, changing a reply, bringing external guests ("+N", counted but not on the team), and what happens when an event is close. Warm, concrete, example-led.

## 10. [RSVP reminders](reminders.md)

How the automatic RSVP reminders work — for both organizers and players. As an event approaches, the assistant nudges players who still haven't replied, on a set schedule, so the organizer doesn't have to chase and players don't forget. Cover when reminders go out, that a player simply receives a text (nothing to install), the limit so no one is over-messaged, and that an organizer can also send a reminder on demand.

## 11. [Headcount: is it on?](headcount.md)

What happens when too many players say yes for the spots (the assistant flags that you're over the limit and offers to ask someone to sit out) or too few say yes (it flags that you're short) — the killer headcount flow. Use those exact framings — "too many for the spots", "over", "short" — and lead with the proactive moment for a scrimmage that's suddenly on or suddenly short. Also cover that the organizer can ask for the full picture anytime ("how's Saturday looking?") and get one warm summary — headcount versus target, the RSVP breakdown, and any sub search in progress. Finally, cover that if an event is still short close to start (about 12 hours out), the assistant checks in with the organizer and offers a clear choice — try to find subs if there's still time, or call it off — leaving the decision to them.

## 12. [Setting up and changing events](events.md)

How a captain or organizer creates, edits, and cancels events by text — use a scrimmage as the running example — and how teammates are automatically kept in the loop on changes and cancellations.

## 13. [Sharing the load: co-organizers and event owners](delegation.md)

How a captain or organizer hands off a single event to whoever's running it, or adds a co-captain or co-organizer with full authority — and how that person is told.

## 14. [Finding subs when you're short](subs.md)

How the assistant finds substitutes across the teams or groups you run when an event is short — opt-in, outreach, who got the spot, and clean close-out.

## 15. [Sharing payment info](pay-info.md)

We help relay who-owes payment info — we don't process or hold money. How an organizer sets payment details for a team or event, how players save their own payment handles, and how the assistant relays the who-owes instructions, confirms, and follows up — so nobody has to chase.
