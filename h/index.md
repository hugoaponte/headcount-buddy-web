# Headcount Buddy help

The front door to the guides — a short, friendly orientation to what the assistant does, then a clean numbered list linking every topic page.

1. **[Getting your team or group on board](teams)** — How a captain or organizer gets their team or group set up. We're in a limited alpha today, so onboarding is hands-on — reach out and we'll get you started. Explain the kinds of groups and sports we support (tennis first; formal teams and informal pickup groups both welcome).
2. **[Your first text from the assistant](start)** — What it's like the very first time the assistant texts you — who it is, what it can help with, and how it catches you up on anything you missed before you arrived. Warm and reassuring; you never install anything.
3. **[Building your roster](roster)** — How a captain or organizer adds teammates so the assistant can reach them. Make explicit that the assistant proactively fills in the details it needs — a teammate's name, gender for mixed-format play — by simply asking them, so the organizer doesn't have to chase that info down.
4. **[Asking your team to RSVP](get-rsvps)** — How a captain or organizer asks the whole team to RSVP for a scrimmage or practice, then nudges anyone who hasn't replied — without @-everyone group chat noise. Lead with how little the organizer has to do.
5. **[Replying to RSVPs](rsvp)** — How a player RSVPs by text — yes/no/maybe plus freeform constraints, changing a reply, reminders, and what happens when an event is close. Warm, concrete, example-led.
6. **[Headcount: is it on?](headcount)** — How the assistant tracks who's in, flags too-many or too-few, and helps fill or trim a spot — the killer flow. Lead with the proactive moment for a scrimmage that's suddenly on, or suddenly short.
7. **[Setting up and changing events](events)** — How a captain or organizer creates, edits, and cancels events by text — use a scrimmage as the running example — and how teammates are automatically kept in the loop on changes and cancellations.
8. **[Sharing the load: co-organizers and event owners](delegation)** — How a captain or organizer hands off a single event to whoever's running it, or adds a co-captain or co-organizer with full authority — and how that person is told.
9. **[Finding subs when you're short](subs)** — How the assistant finds substitutes across the teams or groups you run when an event is short — opt-in, outreach, who got the spot, and clean close-out.
10. **[Sharing payment info](pay-info)** — We help relay who-owes payment info — we don't process or hold money. How an organizer sets payment details for a team or event, how players save their own payment handles, and how the assistant relays the who-owes instructions, confirms, and follows up — so nobody has to chase.
11. **[START, STOP, and INFO](basic-flows)** — The canonical text keywords every teammate can use — START to opt in, STOP to opt out, INFO/HELP for a quick reminder of who we are. Plain, factual, and exact about what each one does.
12. **[What we support: definitions and scope](scope)** — A plain-English glossary of the building blocks — the sports we support (tennis today), the kinds of groups (formal teams and informal pickup), the event types, the gender formats for play, the RSVP states, and the organizer roles. Ground every list in the code so it can't drift.
13. **[Getting help](help)** — How to get unstuck — ask the assistant a question in plain text, share feedback or report a problem, and reach a human when you need one.
